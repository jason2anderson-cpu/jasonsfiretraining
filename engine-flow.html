Engine Evolution

The main pumping evolution. Spot the rig, lock it down, get water on the line, take the hydrant without dropping the attack, and go big when it is called for. The steps are the muscle memory — tap a drop-down for what the manual says behind the step.

Positioning

1

POSITION OUTSIDE COLLAPSE ZONE

Collapse zone — the manual's number

If a stream has to go in front of a building it must be set back far enough that it will not be struck by falling walls or debris. The accepted figure is one and one-half times the height of the tallest exterior wall — a store front with a 20-foot wall gets a 30-foot zone, "the further the better."

Anyone working a stream from the front of the building is standing in that zone and may not have time to react to a sudden collapse of an exterior wall.

Engine Company Operations 2023 V-1, ch.7 Master Stream Operations, pp.7-1–7-2

    Decide the lay while you are still rolling, not at the panel.

    "The Engineer should position the apparatus for a Forward Lay whenever the need for water supply is determined. A hydrant in the immediate vicinity of the fire building may be used if it allows for a rapid stretch and will not interfere with ladder company positioning."

    The first arriving Engine Company Officer makes the call on the need for water supply, or hands that responsibility to the second arriving engine — either way it is transmitted from first due to second due.

    Alarms and investigations: pump in service, no hydrant yet, stay on the panel.

    1st due — nothing showing and alarms
    • Park in a location that allows for easy access by ladder trucks.
    • Fully prepare the engine — complete all steps required to put the pump in service and supply water to hose.
    • No hydrant connection made until otherwise warranted by fire involvement.
    • Stay by the pump and monitor radio traffic for further assignments.

    Engine Company Operations 2023 V-1, p.1-9

  • 2ND DUE — DO NOT BLOCK, AND BE ABLE TO LAY

    Your spot has to leave you able to put a supply line into the first-in engine.

    Manual wording

    "Second arriving engines should position so as not to block any apparatus movement on the fireground and be able to lay a supply line from the first-in engine to the hydrant."

    Engine Company Operations 2023 V-1, p.1-9

    "The Engineer of the second arriving engine is assigned to supply the first engine, this should be accomplished without placing their apparatus at the front of the fire scene. This is best accomplished by laying a supply line away from the first engine, spotting the second engine at the hydrant, connecting to that hydrant, and supplying the first arriving engine."

    Engine Company Operations 2023 V-1, pp.1-10–1-11

  • 3RD AND LATER — OUT OF THE WAY, OR TAKE THE ALLEY

    Behind the structure buys easier additional lines.

    Manual wording

    "Third due engines and later-arriving engines should position their apparatus out of the way of the direct fire scene or consider taking a position in the alley behind the structure to provide additional lines more easily."

    Engine Company Operations 2023 V-1, p.1-9

  • KNOW WHAT ARRIVAL ORDER MAKES YOU

    The riding order sets the assignment before you ever pick a spot.

    Initial company assignments

    Residential and 1–3 floor multi-family: the 1st engine takes size-up and the 360 if not completed, stretches the initial attack line to the fire floor, and initiates water supply if the CO calls for it. The 2nd engine completes water supply, pumps the hydrant for the 1st engine if needed, and stretches the 2nd attack line. The 3rd stretches the back-up line to the 1st engine's location. The 4th stages at a second water supply and takes RIT.

    Commercial moves the FDC to the 3rd engine — position on the FDC and pump it upon a "working fire".

    OFD Quick Reference Guide for Initial Company Operations, May 2024 · full assignment grid

Who is breaking and flaking that line

The Nozzle Firefighter owns the first 100 feet and the nozzle — the lead length. If the Back-Up Firefighter is pulled onto other duties — water supply, forced entry — then "the Nozzle Firefighter is responsible for stretching and flaking the entire hoseline." That is your cue that the stretch is going to take longer.

Engine Company Operations 2023 V-1, p.1-13

The Back-Up Firefighter's hose management is three things: chasing kinks · exterior management of the hose · supporting the movement of hose around corners and through doorways. "Failure of the Back-Up Firefighter to complete these tasks will lead to the delay in the initial attack line progressing to the seat of the fire."

Engine Company Operations 2023 V-1, p.1-17, restated p.1-21

And if a water supply is deemed necessary, the hydrant connection is the Back-Up Firefighter's responsibility — which is exactly when you lose him off the line.

Engine Company Operations 2023 V-1, p.1-21

On an extended lay into apartments, garden-style complexes or long-setback townhomes, the Back-Up Firefighter deploys the 1¾" Modified Minuteman to where it can be stretched near the point of entry, and the Nozzle Firefighter communicates the point of the attack line stretch.

Engine Company Operations 2023 V-1, p.1-14

6

CHARGE LINES

The signal to charge

The line gets flaked at a safe area near the fire area — porch, stairway landing, hallway, adjoining area — and the Nozzle Firefighter finishes donning. When the line is flaked and he is ready he signals the Engineer to charge it. The signal may be verbal, by radio, or by hand signal.

He then bleeds trapped air with the nozzle fully opened and the stream directed away from the opening, and confirms pressure and pattern — a combination tip fully rotated to the straight stream position. "Right to Fight."

Engine Company Operations 2023 V-1, p.1-15

CONNECT LDH SUPPLY

The lay you are on the end of

Orem uses three supply line lays — Forward (lay-in) · Reverse (lay-out) · Split — chosen on arrival order, direction of travel, occupancy type and anticipated water needs, and each can run wet or dry. Whatever the first-in Officer selects, "they MUST announce over the radio their water supply intentions so that the second due engine can adjust their strategies."

Engine Company Operations 2023 V-1, p.5-1

Wet lead: the Back-Up Firefighter stays at the hydrant and charges the line when told. Dry lead: the Tailboard Firefighter anchors the hose, leaves the hydrant bucket, and goes to the fire with the crew — someone else is assigned to charge it.

Engine Company Operations 2023 V-1, p.5-8

Reverse lay splits two ways: a supply evolution (LDH between the suction side of the pump and the hydrant) or an attack evolution (LDH off the discharge side to an appliance or master stream). Split lay is for when the first due has to park where there is no water — cul-de-sac, limited-access complex — dropping LDH at the entrance for the second due to complete. The first due must transmit the intention to split.

Engine Company Operations 2023 V-1, pp.5-9–5-11

"When performing a Forward Lay, the connection shall be made using 5" LDH, in the event the 5" LDH connection cannot be secured on the hydrant a minimum of 2-3" hoses shall be connected, one on each side of the hydrant. Pony lines are acceptable where connections are short as long as '5" LDH first' rules are applied."

Engine Company Operations 2023 V-1, p.1-11 · more in Water Supply

How much water that line is worth

A single 5-inch line may not flow all of the available water from a hydrant or main. Provide one 5-inch hose line for every 1,000 gpm to be delivered.

A double 2½" hydrant — two 2½" outlets, no steamer — is fed by a 4" main; expect less than 600 gpm.

Engine Company Operations 2023 V-1, pp.5-4–5-5

To pull more out of the distribution system, least to best preferred: multiple supply lines from a single hydrant · pumping the hydrant with the 2nd or 3rd due engine supplying the attack pumper · multiple supply lines from different hydrants sharing the same water main · multiple supply lines from different hydrants not sharing the same main.

Engine Company Operations 2023 V-1, p.5-13 — High-Flow Hydrant Operations · relay spacing and pressures in Relay Pumping

SIGNAL FOR WATER

What is happening at the hydrant

Orem's hydrants are dry-barrel. Once a dry-barrel hydrant is charged, no additional lines may be added to the other outlets unless the hydrant is shut down or a shut-off valve was added before charging. Opening the stem valve supplies ALL outlets at the same time — so multi-outlet dry barrels must have a 2½" gate valve attached to an unused outlet at the onset of any hydrant operation.

Stem valves open counter-clockwise — "open-left" — across every district around Orem.

Engine Company Operations 2023 V-1, pp.5-2 and 5-5

The hydrant bucket carries what the connection needs: screw-handle hydrant wrench, 5" Storz × 2½" female, 2½" and Storz spanners, two 2½" hydrant gate valves, a 2½" double-female for a 3" supply line, and a rubber mallet for stuck caps. Depending on the lay, the 1st or 2nd due engine leaves it at the hydrant.

Engine Company Operations 2023 V-1, p.5-6

Orem's own hydrant sequence: stretch LDH with the gate valve and hydrant bag · connect LDH to the steamer port and attach both gate valves to the 2½" outlets · flush the hydrant · open the hydrant slowly until the hose is charged · close the intake bleeder valve · open the intake valve · close tank to pump · mark or note the intake gauge · open tank fill to refill or confirm the tank is full · verbalize supply is established.

ADO-Pumper task book, Evolution 1.0 — out-of-cab hydrant/water supply steps 4–13

OPEN SUPPLY INTAKE SLOWLY (MONITOR GAUGES)

Changeover doctrine — ADO Task #19

Purge the air first. Air sitting in an empty supply hose causes problems if it enters the pump while the pump is supplying attack lines under pressure. Open the bleeder valve on the gated intake so the air escapes as the line fills; when all the air has been forced out, close the bleeder. The water is now at the pump and ready for the transition.

Then the transition. "The operator opens the intake valve from the supply line very slowly while observing the pressure on the intake and discharge gauges." Keep opening the intake and adjusting the discharge pressure until the intake is completely open — it may take throttle to hold the discharge pressure you want.

Then close tank-to-pump. After the intake valve is open the tank-to-pump valve can be closed — but leave the tank fill partially open until the tank has refilled. "A full tank provides a limited reserve water supply in the event that the external water supply is lost unexpectedly."

ADO Task #19, Basic Pump Skills — Changeover (Transition to an External Water Supply)

On the drill ground the standard is that the changeover has minimal impact on the hand lines — approximately 10 psi or less.

MASTER STREAM

1

PROPERLY SECURE DEVICE

Where the device goes, and what it does to the ground

Master streams generally mean interior offensive operations have been abandoned for a defensive posture. Once the line is in operation the device must be shut down to be repositioned — so the position has to be right the first time.

The safest exterior positions are the corners of the building. Setting up in front, whether off the top of the engine or off the ground, "is a dangerous and ineffective tactic" — the crew is in the collapse zone.

Two more things the higher flow does to you: it is more likely to knock debris loose from the building and throw it at people and apparatus; and in portable mode the device has a limited side-to-side range — turning the nozzle too far left or right can make the base unstable and tip it over.

Engine Company Operations 2023 V-1, ch.7, p.7-1

The Stinger is a break-apart monitor that works as a deck gun or as a portable — plumbed to an outlet on top of the engine so it goes into operation without running a supply line, or pulled off and dropped onto its base, which has folding legs and a single 5" LDH inlet and stows in the rear compartment. Going portable is how you get the fire away from the apparatus.

Engine Company Operations 2023 V-1, pp.7-4–7-5 · Master Streams

5

OPEN DISCHARGE

Deck gun geometry — where that water lands

A deck gun nozzle sitting nine to ten feet above the ground loses a lot of effectiveness at low angles. On a narrow street most of the water is wasted — the stream hits the floor only 25 to 30 feet inside the structure.

The most effective use of the pre-connected deck gun is a quick application of high-pressure water through the window to knock down heavy fire while other members get a handline into position by whatever means available. Faster fire control means increased life safety for civilians and firefighters.

At upper-floor angles the stream is less likely to push fire through the compartment and out into the hallway — most of the water strikes the ceiling and coarse droplets rain down on the burning contents. A smaller tip than normally used, and limiting the application, reduces that risk further; a 1½" tip generates the high velocity needed for reach and conserves water.

Engine Company Operations 2023 V-1, ch.7, pp.7-2–7-3

Orem's Class B foam math

Field method. Hydrocarbons: area ÷ 20. Polar solvents / alcohols: area ÷ 5. That gives gallons of concentrate.

Full formula. Area × Critical Application Rate × Eduction Rate × 15 minutes = concentrate needed. CAR is 0.1 gpm/sq ft for hydrocarbons, 0.2 for polars. The eduction rate — the minimum metering valve setting — is 1–3% for hydrocarbons, 3–6% for polars. The 15 minutes is the NFPA 11 standard for concentrate on hand.

Worked example. A 10' × 10' gasoline spill: 100 × 0.1 × 0.03 × 15 = 4.5 gallons. Field method: 100 ÷ 20 = 5 gallons.

In-line eductors. Rated at 95 gpm — the eductor and the nozzle must have matching flow rates or the concentrate will not pick up. Designed for a 200 psi inlet. Do not exceed 70% back pressure beyond the eductor.

OFD Hydraulic Standard — Foam Operations §1–2

Field notes — this sheet vs. Orem's printed evolutions

Evolution 1.0 has no chock step.

Orem's Evolution 1.0 skill sheet runs its out-of-cab list from "verify pump is engaged" straight into the hydrant work — no wheel chocks anywhere in it. Evolutions 1.1 through 1.5 all carry "Set wheel chocks" as out-of-cab step 6, and ADO Task #19 opens by immobilising the rig with the parking brake and the chocks. The step belongs on 1.0 too. Committee item.

The relief valve is printed last, and set early.

The drill-ground evolutions list the manual discharge relief valve as the final out-of-cab item. ADO Task #19 sets the relief valve or pressure governor at step 6 — before the external supply is introduced. This sheet follows #19: set it before pressure reaches the line.
