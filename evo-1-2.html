Out-of-cab — changeover to hydrant supply

Printed steps 12–18, with the muscle-memory steps merged in where they belong. The + steps are not on the printed sheet.

+

SET DISCHARGE RELIEF VALVE TO ~150

Why
“…then when I introduce hydrant water there isn't as much of a pressure spike and the discharge relief valve will catch any extra. Then after hydrant is fully opened I can throttle back down.”

Engineer Anderson, 2026-08-30 review. The changeover task agrees on the order: “Step 6: Set the relief valve or pressure governor” comes before “Step 8: When an external supply becomes available, open the intake valve” — the printed sheet leaves its relief-valve step until step 18, after the changeover is done.

+

THROTTLE UP TO 150 PSI

+

GATE DOWN HANDLINES TO THEIR SET PDP

Each line keeps flowing at its correct pressure while the pump carries the higher pre-set.

+

CONNECT LDH SUPPLY

+

OPEN PRESSURE RELIEF VALVE

Why & source — purge the air first
“Air in the empty supply hose will cause problems if it enters the pump while the pump is supplying attack lines under pressure. To purge this air, open the bleeder valve on the gated intake line so that the air can escape as the supply line fills with water.”

ADO Task #19 Basic Pump Skills-Changeover, opening narrative

The printed 1.2 sheet never opens the bleeder — it only closes it (step 13), and only after the intake is already open. Evolution 1.0 has the correct order: close the intake bleeder valve before opening the intake. This sheet carries the missing step.

+

SIGNAL FOR WATER

+

CLOSE PRESSURE RELIEF VALVE

When
“When all the air has been forced from the line, close the bleeder valve. The water is now at the pump and ready for the transition.”

ADO Task #19 Basic Pump Skills-Changeover, opening narrative

12

Open intake from hydrant slowly, make sure Pressure governor adjusts pump pressure or manually adjust pressure if needed.

Why & source — why slowly
“To begin the transition, the operator opens the intake valve from the supply line very slowly while observing the pressure on the intake and discharge gauges. Start opening intake valve and adjusting the discharge pressure until the intake is completely open. It may be necessary to adjust the engine throttle to get the desire discharge pressure.”

ADO Task #19 Basic Pump Skills-Changeover, opening narrative — quoted as printed. The drill standard: hand lines feel approximately 10 psi of change or less.

+

THROTTLE BACK DOWN (ONCE HYDRANT IS FULLY OPEN)

13

Close intake bleeder valve and tank to pump

Ordering note

With the bleeder already handled during the air purge above, the working action left in this printed step is closing tank to pump. The changeover task pairs that with the intake:

“Step 8: When an external supply becomes available, open the intake valve while closing the tank-to-pump valve (if necessary).”

ADO Task #19 Basic Pump Skills-Changeover, Operating Procedure Step 8

14

Check discharge pressure and adjust if needed

+

REFILL TANK

Why & source — the tank is your reserve
“However, the (tank-fill) valve should be left partially open until the water tank has been refilled. Note: A full tank provides a limited reserve water supply in the event that the external water supply is lost unexpectedly.”

ADO Task #19 Basic Pump Skills-Changeover, opening narrative; Step 8 adds “divert enough of it through the tank-fill line to replenish the supply in the tank.” The printed 1.2 sheet has no refill step — the tank-fill valve is only cracked back at step 3 and never revisited. Evolution 1.0 carries it: “Open tank fill to refill or confirm tank is full.”

+

*NOTE STATIC HYDRANT PRESSURE*

15

Open second discharge and set to correct pressure

The second attack line must be different from the first — that is the drill.

16

Mark intake gauge

+

MAINTAIN 20 PSI INTAKE PRESSURE

Why & source — what the intake gauge is telling you

20 psi minimum protects from drawing a vacuum on the intake hose.

The manual gives the gauge a second job on this evolution — it is how you know whether you can add the next line:

“They should also monitor the residual pressure of the pump master intake gauge to determine the capability of supplying additional volumes when available or as needed.”

Engine Company Operations, Ch.1 Firefighter Assignments, p.1-11; intake minimum per Engineer Anderson's evolution card. Carried on Evolution 1.0 as a printed step.

17

Verbalize supply is established

18

Set manual discharge relief valve

Manual valve: Engine 31 · Engine 3820 · Ladder 32

What ADO Task #19 teaches about the changeover

Task #19 — Basic Pump Skills-Changeover (Transition to an External Water Supply) — is the department's written doctrine for exactly this evolution. Quoted in full, as printed.

The changeover, in the task's own words
“Transition from the water tank to an external water supply source most commonly involves the use of a pressurized source. This will be a hose-line being supplied by another apparatus or from a fire hydrant.”
“Air in the empty supply hose will cause problems if it enters the pump while the pump is supplying attack lines under pressure. To purge this air, open the bleeder valve on the gated intake line so that the air can escape as the supply line fills with water. When all the air has been forced from the line, close the bleeder valve. The water is now at the pump and ready for the transition.”
“To begin the transition, the operator opens the intake valve from the supply line very slowly while observing the pressure on the intake and discharge gauges. Start opening intake valve and adjusting the discharge pressure until the intake is completely open. It may be necessary to adjust the engine throttle to get the desire discharge pressure. After the intake valve is opened, the tank-to-pump valve can be closed. However, the (tank-fill) valve should be left partially open until the water tank has been refilled. Note: A full tank provides a limited reserve water supply in the event that the external water supply is lost unexpectedly.”

ADO Task #19 Basic Pump Skills-Changeover, opening narrative — quoted verbatim, including the printed spellings

Operating Procedure for Transition to an External Water Supply — steps 1–9
  1. “Position the apparatus in a safe positon, and immobilize it by setting the parking brake and chocking the wheels.”
  2. “Engage the pump, and select the proper gear in the road transmission. Lock the gear into place.”
  3. “Open the tank-to-pump valve (if necessary).”
  4. “Set the transfer valve to the series (to PSI or RMP) positon depending on application.”
  5. “Increase the throttle setting to obtain the desired pressure, priming (if necessary).”
  6. “Set the relief valve or pressure governor.”
  7. “Open the circulator valve or partially open the tank fill valve.”
  8. “When an external supply becomes available, open the intake valve while closing the tank-to-pump valve (if necessary). As soon as an adequate supply of water is available, divert enough of it through the tank-fill line to replenish the supply in the tank.”
  9. “Adjust the engine throttle to achieve the desired discharge pressure (if necessary).”

ADO Task #19 Basic Pump Skills-Changeover, Operating Procedure — quoted verbatim, including the printed spellings

Where the printed 1.2 sheet and Task #19 differ

Bleeder valve. Task #19 opens the intake bleeder before the transition so air escapes as the supply line fills, and closes it once water is at the pump. The printed 1.2 sheet never opens it and closes it only after the intake is open (step 13). Evolution 1.0 has the correct order.

Tank refill. Task #19 requires the tank to be replenished once supply is adequate, as a reserve against losing the external supply. The printed 1.2 sheet has no refill step; Evolution 1.0 carries one.

Relief valve timing. Task #19 sets the relief valve or governor (Step 6) before the intake is opened (Step 8). The printed 1.2 sheet leaves “Set manual discharge relief valve” as its final step (18), after the changeover is complete and two lines are flowing.

Tank-to-pump timing. Task #19 closes tank-to-pump while the intake opens; the printed sheet fully opens the intake first (step 12), then closes tank-to-pump (step 13).

ADO Task #19 Basic Pump Skills-Changeover vs. ADO-Pumper Task Book, Evolution 1.2 (September 2024 Version). The merged sequence above resolves each of these on the sheet.

What the manuals teach about supplying various hose loads

Engine Company Operations Ch.1 (Firefighter Assignments) and Ch.5 (Water Supply) are the doctrine behind the two halves of this evolution — carrying several lines at once, and getting off the tank onto a hydrant. Page numbers are the manual's own.

What the Engineer must know to carry more than one line
“In order to establish and maintain proper pumping pressures, all Engineers must know how many hoselines, the size and length of the hoseline, the type of nozzles being used, and their flow.”
“It is imperative that the Engineer listen closely to the radio at all times for traffic concerning charging, shutting down or adjusting hose line pressures on hose lines.”

Engine Company Operations, Ch.1 Firefighter Assignments, p.1-11

“The Engineer must control how many and what type streams and hose lines are supplied and should confer with and advise the officer when necessary.”
“Must know how many hose lines, size, length, nozzles, and the flow rates of their apparatus. (per OFD hydraulic standard)”

Engine Company Operations, Ch.1 Firefighter Assignments, p.1-8 — Engineer / ADO (Driver), description and Responsibility 3

This is why steps 10, 11 and 15 exist as separate actions: two different hose loads, two different pressures, each one marked or noted so it can be re-set after the changeover.

Reading the intake gauge — residual pressure and additional volume
“They should also monitor the residual pressure of the pump master intake gauge to determine the capability of supplying additional volumes when available or as needed.”

Engine Company Operations, Ch.1 Firefighter Assignments, p.1-11

Printed step 16 — “Mark intake gauge” — is that reading. Noting the static pressure before the intake is opened and the residual after it is what tells you whether the second line, and any line after it, is there to be had.

Why the evolution starts on the tank at all
“Most fires can be controlled with the available water carried in the booster tank. However, for redundancy and in those cases where supplement water is required for prolonged operations the engine company relies on the municipal water system.”
“This doesn’t eliminate the need to use the on-board booster tank to supply the initial fire attack, but rather limits the amount of water that will have to be used while waiting for the hydrant water to arrive.”

Engine Company Operations, Ch.5 Water Supply, p.5-1

The changeover is the moment that hand-off happens. The drill standard — approximately 10 psi or less felt at the hand lines — is the measure of doing it without the crew noticing.

Dry-barrel hydrants — gate the spare outlets before you charge
“It is important to remember, when a dry-barrel hydrant is charged no additional lines may be added to the other outlets unless the hydrant is shut down or a shut off valve is added prior to charging.”
“To accommodate additional water supply needs, multi-outlet dry barrel hydrants must have a 2 1/2” hydrant gate-valve attached to an unused outlet at the onset of any hydrant operation.”

Engine Company Operations, Ch.5 Water Supply, p.5-2 — Dry-Barrel

On a dry barrel the stem valve feeds every outlet at once, so the gate valves go on before the water does. Once the hydrant is charged the decision is made for you.

“The nationally recognized direction to open hydrant stem valves is counter-clockwise(“Open-Left”).”

Engine Company Operations, Ch.5 Water Supply, p.5-5 — Stem Valve Rotation

Who charges the supply line when you signal for water
“The ‘Wet’ Lead: The back-up firefighter will remain at the hydrant and charge the line when the command to do so is received.”
“The ‘Dry’ Lead: The Tailboard firefighter anchors the hose, leaves the hydrant bucket, and proceeds to the fire with his/her crew. Other personnel will be assigned to charge the hydrant.”

Engine Company Operations, Ch.5 Water Supply, p.5-8 — Forward Lay, operational modes

Which of the two you are working decides whether your signal for water reaches someone already standing at the hydrant, or has to be assigned first. On a dry lead the water is further away than the hose suggests.

“Whatever decision the first-in Company Officer chooses to select, they MUST announce over the radio their water supply intentions so that the second due engine can adjust their strategies accordantly.”

Engine Company Operations, Ch.5 Water Supply, p.5-1 — quoted as printed

Quick Reference Guide — whose job the supply is

1st Engine Company, residential: “Size-up & 360 if not completed. Stretch initial attack line to fire floor. Initiate water supply if determined by CO.”

2nd Engine Company, residential: “Complete water supply, Pump hydrant for 1st engine if needed. Stretch 2nd attack line (Outside, Floor above fire, Basement).”

OFD Quick Reference Guide for Initial Company Operations, May 2024 — Engine Company Functions

Evolution 1.2 is the first-due version of that: initiate your own supply while your lines are already flowing off the tank. The guide carries its own caveat — “This document is a quick reference document and is not designed to override or replace understanding of the full applicable Manuals.”
