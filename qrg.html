Where the second line goes, and why

“The objective of the first hoseline will determine where the Second line is placed.” Interior extinguishment, egress protection or exterior knockdown — that call decides the position. The manual notes it may go in as an exterior attack line while the first line works inside, protect the stairwell while the first line works a basement, or sit on an upper floor against vertical extension. “The second hoseline shall be coordinated with the first hoseline and positioned accordingly.”

Back-up line rules — the whole list

The back-up line is “usually the second or third line that is stretched.” Its job is to protect the crews inside, and Orem prints hard rules for it:

  • Stretch it to or through the same entrance as the attack line — that covers the attack team’s whole path.
  • Do not let it delay the initial attack line. Make sure the attack line is kink free and pushing toward the seat of the fire first; if it is not moving, help them advance.
  • Do not fight the fire with it. If you get into the fire you lose sight of protecting the people above you.
  • Protect stairwells and other main vertical channels of fire spread.
  • It must be at least as long as the attack line, and the same diameter or larger.
  • It needs a continuous water supply — it cannot protect anyone if it runs out of water.
  • It is not the RIT team’s line. RIT has its own dedicated hose line.

Situations it exists for: an additional attack line is needed · pressure loss to the attack line · the attack team is trapped by fire · a search team’s egress is cut off. If it does get used as an additional attack line, that has to be transmitted to the IC so another line takes over the back-up job.

How the Truck splits, and what you say on the radio

Truck functions are split into three geographic areas — Topside, Outside and Inside teams. The Truck officer “has the option to ‘split’ and accomplish simultaneous assignments in accordance with the OFD Quick Reference Guide for Initial Company Operations (MAY 2024)” — this page.

On arrival the Truck Captain announces three things: 1) which due Truck they are, 2) ability to split, 3) where and what they are splitting to accomplish. The expectation is that Truck companies split whenever possible.

OFD Truck Manual v3.1, Section 3, pp.8–9 · Truck Evolution

Truck priorities on a single family dwelling

In order: 1. Ventilation · 2. Search · 3. Forcible Entry/Egress · 4. Ladders · 5. Utilities · 6. Salvage · 7. Overhaul.

“The first arriving Truck on a single family dwelling should assume the following assignments, Ventilation and Search. If the first arriving Truck Captain cannot split his crew due to staffing, the remaining assignments should be assumed by the 2nd Due Truck.” That is exactly the shape of the two Truck rows in this column.

Geographic split: Inside — search, overhaul, salvage. Outside — ventilation, VES, forcible entry/egress, ladders, utilities. Topside — ventilation.

OFD Truck Manual v3.1, Section 4, p.13

Why the mid-rise column hands you the standpipe

Standpipe systems “will be found in multifamily residential buildings over three floors in height, and commercial or residential low rise and high rise buildings.” That is the same line the Quick Reference Guide draws at four floors. On a standpipe building the Engine Company owes three things: get hose, nozzles, fittings and tools to the outlet valve; stretch and advance the line; and supply or augment the system so there is adequate volume at proper pressure.

Engine Company Operations 2023 V-1, p.6-1

Supplying the system — the manual makes this the 2nd due’s first critical task

“The first critical task facing the 2nd due Engine Companies arriving at a fire in a standpipe-equipped building is to establish a water supply by pumping into the FDC connections. 3″ hose should be used to supply all standpipe systems.”

The second engine takes a position at the appropriate FDC standpipe connection, supplies two 3″ lines into the FDC Siamese and connects to the nearest hydrant. The third arriving engine relay pumps to the second from the next nearest hydrant, and later engines continue that pattern where additional FDC connections exist — on a big building, often on different street fronts.

Two engines per Siamese is deliberate: it gives an independent and redundant supply if a pumper fails mechanically or the FDC feeds only a single stairwell riser.

Truck priorities on a center-hallway apartment

Center-hallway apartments are low to mid-rise structures where every apartment shares one interior hallway for access and egress, with no exterior stairwells or walkways. They “have historically experienced some of the greatest losses of life due to the interior public hallway being overcome with smoke and fire.”

Priorities in order: 1. Ventilation · 2. Search · 3. Ladders · 4. Forcible Entry/Egress · 5. Utilities · 6. Overhaul · 7. Salvage.

Inside search runs stairwell → fire floor hallway → apartment of origin → adjacent apartments → floor above → top floor. That is the same ladder of floors the Truck rows in this column hand out: 1st Truck the fire floor, 2nd Truck the floor above, 3rd Truck the top floor.

What the officer owes the radio before anything else

Orem’s first-due list is fixed: transmit the report on conditions using the OFD Initial Size-up from the Tactical Worksheet — unit on scene, repeat the address, describe the building and the conditions, initial operations, declare side A, state who has Command and Accountability, name the incident, declare Strategy (Offensive / Defensive) and Mode (Investigation / Fast-Action / Command). Then the after-size-up items, and “Conduct or Assign 360” is one of them.

Engine Company Operations 2023 V-1, p.1-4

Water supply is a decision that has to be said out loud

The officer has four ways to take supply — “lay-in wet, lay-in dry, reverse lay, or hand-jack” — and if they cannot take it they MUST notify the next incoming company to lay in a water supply.

Engine Company Operations 2023 V-1, p.1-3

Chapter 5 says the same thing from the other end: the first engine may pass up a water supply, attack off the tank and assign the supply line to the second due — but “Whatever decision the first-in Company Officer chooses to select, they MUST announce over the radio their water supply intentions so that the second due engine can adjust their strategies accordantly.” That radio traffic is what makes the row below yours work.

Engine Company Operations 2023 V-1, p.5-1 · Water supply

Back-up line rules — the whole list

The back-up line is “usually the second or third line that is stretched.” Its job is to protect the crews inside, and Orem prints hard rules for it:

  • Stretch it to or through the same entrance as the attack line — that covers the attack team’s whole path.
  • Do not let it delay the initial attack line. Make sure the attack line is kink free and pushing toward the seat of the fire first; if it is not moving, help them advance.
  • Do not fight the fire with it. If you get into the fire you lose sight of protecting the people above you.
  • Protect stairwells and other main vertical channels of fire spread.
  • It must be at least as long as the attack line, and the same diameter or larger.
  • It needs a continuous water supply — it cannot protect anyone if it runs out of water.
  • It is not the RIT team’s line. RIT has its own dedicated hose line.

Situations it exists for: an additional attack line is needed · pressure loss to the attack line · the attack team is trapped by fire · a search team’s egress is cut off. If it does get used as an additional attack line, that has to be transmitted to the IC so another line takes over the back-up job.

Engine Company Operations 2023 V-1, pp.4-7–4-8

“Third due engines and later-arriving engines should position their apparatus out of the way of the direct fire scene or consider taking a position in the alley behind the structure to provide additional lines more easily.”

Heads up — the FDC question

This box puts the FDC on the 3rd engine and conditions pumping it on a “working fire” declaration. Engine Company Operations makes supplying the FDC the 2nd due engine’s first critical task on a standpipe building, with the 3rd relay pumping to it. On a standpipe building the two documents agree it is the 2nd due; they part company on a commercial building. Ask a chief which one governs a commercial FDC.

Wording items for the training committee

  • Mid-rise, 4th Engine — “lay line to cover side opposite side”

    Published on this page exactly as the guide prints it. The Commercial box on the same row reads “lay line to cover side Charlie,” so the mid-rise wording is most likely meant to name a side too. It is not a spelling slip — it changes what you are being told to cover, so it is not something this page fixes on its own. Ask for the intended wording.

  • “Ladder Drivers will remain with the rig to set aerial device”

    This note appears three times in the mid-rise column — on the 1st, 2nd and 3rd Truck rows — and nowhere else in the guide. It sits against the Truck Manual’s riding positions, where the Engineer is Lead Saw and responsible for roof operations. Needs context from a chief: does it apply to every mid-rise, or only where the aerial is actually going up?

    OFD Truck Manual v3.1, Section 3, pp.10–11

  • “All In” and “All Out” are used but never defined

    Both terms appear only in the mid-rise Truck rows and the guide does not say what triggers either one, or who declares it. Two different assignments hang off which one is in effect — on the 2nd and 3rd Truck rows, “All Out” sends a crew to the roof instead of to a floor. Worth a definition in the next revision.

  • Commercial FDC — 3rd engine here, 2nd engine in the manual

    This guide puts the FDC on the 3rd Engine and conditions pumping it on a “working fire” declaration. Engine Company Operations makes supplying the FDC the 2nd due engine’s first critical task on a standpipe-equipped building, with the 3rd relay pumping to it. On a standpipe building both documents land on the 2nd due; they only split on a commercial building. Question for a chief and captain.

    OFD Quick Reference Guide, May 2024 · Engine Company Operations 2023 V-1, p.6-16

  • Empty boxes in the Residential column

    The guide prints nothing for the 5th Engine, the 3rd Truck or Heavy Rescue on a residential or 1–3 floor multi-family building. That may be deliberate — those units are unlikely to be on a first alarm there — but the page shows the blanks rather than guessing, so nobody reads an assignment into them.
